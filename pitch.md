# Pitch: Little Grocery Store

- **Genre:** Hybrid-casual store-building game with chance-based mini-games
- **Platform:** Android
- **Format:** Portrait, playable with one thumb
- **Session length:** 1–3 minutes

## Core Loop

Players build their own grocery store by collecting products through short chance-based mini-games and arranging them on shelves.

A prize wheel, a Slot Machine, and scratch cards reveal different product types and quantities. For example, a spin might award five milk cartons, while a scratch card might reveal a new product for the player's collection.

Players then place the products on shelves, choose how to arrange their store, and work toward completing shelves and sections. Completing these goals rewards coins and XP.

The loop is: play a mini-game → receive products → arrange and fill shelves → complete goals → earn coins and XP → expand the store.

The main objective is to fill and develop the store. There is no move-limit sorting puzzle or customer-service requirement.

## Meta

Players spend coins to unlock additional shelves and purchase store upgrades, creating more space for their growing product collection.

XP increases the store level and unlocks new product types and store sections. Mini-games award products from the currently unlocked selection, including opportunities to discover products the player has not collected yet.

Products, shelf arrangements, currency, XP, and upgrades are saved locally between sessions.

## Why Come Back?

- Discover new products through surprise rewards.
- Collect the products needed to complete unfinished shelves.
- Earn enough coins to unlock the next shelf or upgrade.
- Reach the next store level and unlock a new section.
- Watch an empty grocery store become a full, personalized shop.

## Who and When?

The game targets casual players who enjoy collecting, arranging spaces, and surprise rewards.

It is designed for short breaks, commuting, and relaxing. A typical session lasts 1–3 minutes and includes playing mini-games, placing collected products, and progressing toward a store goal.

## Goals

1. **Fill a shelf:** Collect and place enough products to fill its available spaces.
2. **Complete a section:** Fill the required shelves in a store section.
3. **Discover products:** Collect newly unlocked product types.
4. **Expand the store:** Earn coins and XP to unlock shelves, upgrades, and sections.

Progress is measured through filled shelf spaces, completed sections, discovered products, and purchased upgrades.

## Feel

The game should feel playful, relaxing, and rewarding.

Spinning a wheel or scratching a card creates anticipation, while discovering a new product provides a moment of surprise. Placing products gives satisfying visual and sound feedback.

The store provides a visible record of progress: empty shelves gradually fill with colorful products, and new sections create fresh collection goals.

## Initial Semester Scope

For our three-person team, we propose:

- One store screen with drag-and-drop product placement.
- Three short product-reward mini-games: a prize wheel, a roulette-style spinner, and a scratch card.
- Four store progression milestones with increasingly demanding shelf-filling goals.
- A coin and XP progression system.
- Unlockable shelves, product types, and store upgrades.
- Local saving of the product collection, shelf arrangements, and progression.

The final scope will be agreed with the instructor after the pitch. The mini-games use virtual rewards only; real-money purchases and cash prizes are outside the semester scope.

Our engineering focus will be a shared reward system, data-driven product and probability configuration, reusable mini-game interfaces, inventory management, shelf placement, progression, and local saving. Simple or free art assets will be used.
